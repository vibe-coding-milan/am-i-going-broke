Overview

Am I Going Broke? is a personal cash-flow and budgeting projection tool made by an independent developer, Milan Mijatovic, at amigoingbroke.me. This policy covers the website and its optional Google Drive backup feature. Your financial plan is stored locally in your browser by default. Manual backups transfer directly between your browser and Google Drive. If you choose the optional automatic sync feature when available, the secure sync service handles your plan during transfers as explained below.

Am I Going Broke? is not a bank, lender, investment adviser, tax adviser, or financial adviser.

Information you provide

The app may handle the following information when you use it:

  • Financial planning data you enter, such as balances, income, expenses, recurring bills, payment dates, and projection settings.
  • Browser preferences and backup metadata, such as theme choice, Google Drive connection status, backup file ID, and last backup time.
  • For manual Drive backup, a short-lived Google access token held in page memory. Automatic sync also uses the Google account identifier, verified email address, and server-held Google credentials described below.

Please do not enter highly sensitive credentials or identifiers such as bank passwords, full account numbers, credit card numbers, Social Security numbers, or tax IDs.

How information is used

Your information is used only to create and display your cash-flow projection, remember your local preferences, and perform backup or restore actions that you request.

The app does not sell personal information or use your financial data for advertising, profiling, or marketing. It does not currently use advertising trackers or analytics.

Not financial advice

Projections are estimates based on the information and settings you provide. They are for personal planning purposes only and may be incomplete or inaccurate. Do not treat them as financial, investment, legal, accounting, or tax advice.

Google Drive access

Manual Google Drive backup is optional. Choosing “Export to Google Drive” or “Import from Google Drive…” in the Data menu starts Google authorization when needed. You choose your Google Account and grant access on Google's authorization screen. Each person's backups are saved in their own Drive. The app does not receive your Google password.

Permission requested. The app requests only the https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.file scope. This limits access to files the app creates or files you explicitly make available to it. The current backup feature searches for and reads or updates its own backup files; it does not request access to your entire Drive, Gmail, contacts, calendar, or Google profile.

Data accessed and purpose. On export, the app creates or updates a JSON file named am-i-going-broke-data.json. It contains your financial plan, including balances, payment names and amounts, income and expense schedules, dates, projection settings, a backup format version, and the export time. On import, the app downloads that file, checks its format, and asks for confirmation before replacing your local plan. It also uses backup file IDs and app-specific file metadata to find its backups. This Google user data is used only to provide the backup and restore actions you request. These manual backups are separate from the optional automatic sync working file described below.

Storage and sharing for manual browser authorization. Backup contents travel directly between your browser and Google Drive over HTTPS. Google stores the backup in your Drive under your account's controls and Google's policies. Restored financial data is saved in your browser. The app stores the backup file ID, last backup time, and a connection indicator locally. Google access tokens are held only in the page's memory, are sent to Google to authorize Drive requests, and are not saved in browser storage or on the website server. Reloading or closing the page clears the app's in-memory token; this does not revoke the permission you granted to Google.

With manual browser authorization, the developer does not receive or retain your Drive backup contents or tokens on a server. The app does not automatically share your backup with the developer or other users. Google receives the data necessary for authorization and the requested Drive transfers. The app does not sell Google user data or provide it to advertisers or data brokers, use it for credit or lending decisions, or use it to develop, improve, or train AI or machine-learning models. There is no routine developer review of your financial plan or Drive files. If you choose to email information for support, the developer receives what you send; please do not send financial backups or tokens.

The app's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy , including the Limited Use requirements.

Optional automatic Drive sync

Automatic sync is off by default and requires explicit setup when the feature is available. Authorizing manual Drive backups does not enable it. Setup requests your Google account identifier and verified email address to identify the connected account, and limited drive.file access to manage app-created files. It also requests offline access so the server can refresh the connection without repeatedly interrupting you. Your Google password is never received by the app.

When enabled, your valid plan changes save in this browser and are sent to the sync service after you pause editing. The service passes the plan to one working file, Current plan.json, in an Am I Going Broke? Drive folder. It also reads that file to bring newer changes from your connected devices into this browser. Sample scenarios are excluded from automatic uploads. Separate manual Drive exports through this connection create timestamped files in the folder's Backups subfolder; legacy backups remain discoverable.

The website's hosting provider processes these HTTPS requests. The service temporarily handles your plan in server memory during transfers; it does not maintain a server database of financial plans. It stores encrypted Google access and refresh credentials, your email address, the account identifier, Drive file/folder identifiers, and limited operational metadata outside the publicly served website. The browser receives a secure, HTTP-only session cookie rather than Google's refresh token. Google receives the plan and metadata needed to provide Drive storage. This data is used only for the requested sync, backup, restore, and account-connection functions; it is not sold or used for advertising, credit decisions, or AI training.

Pending sync changes and recovery information are stored in browser storage, separately for each connected account and tab. They create no downloaded files. The working Drive file retains up to three previous synced versions; older internal recovery versions are replaced as new changes are saved. A local recovery slot preserves the plan before a confirmed reset or restore, and conflict recovery preserves both candidates. These browser records remain until site storage is cleared. Manual backups are retained until you delete them, independently of automatic recovery retention. Google's own file-version and trash retention rules also apply.

Turning the toggle off stops automatic transfers on this device but keeps your local plan, Drive files, and saved server connection. A transfer already accepted by the service may finish. Choosing Disconnect all devices removes the server's saved connection credentials and account record and pauses automatic sync for this account on other devices. Browser session records expire after up to 30 days of inactivity, subject to hosting cleanup. To revoke Google's authorization itself, use your Google Account connections. Delete Drive files, downloaded backups, and each device's site storage separately.

Restoring a backup or starting over while sync is on can replace the plan on connected devices; the app asks you to confirm. If both local and Drive plans have changed, it preserves both candidates for a choice. Changes not yet uploaded when a browser closes remain local and require a later visit to sync. As with manual backups, the app does not add password encryption to local plans or Drive files; protect your device, Google Account, and sharing settings.

Local storage and retention

Your plan and preferences remain in this browser's local storage until you change or remove them, or the browser clears them. There is no automatic retention deadline. The app does not maintain a server copy from which the developer can recover your plan. Downloaded backups remain wherever you save them until you delete them.

Drive backups remain in your Google Drive until you delete them. Export may replace the existing backup after your confirmation; the app does not automatically expire old backups. Google's trash and file-history retention rules apply to copies held by Google. Deleting local data, revoking Google access, and deleting a Drive backup are separate actions; none automatically performs the others.

Data protection. The website and Drive API requests use HTTPS to protect data in transit. Drive permissions are limited to the scope described above, and access tokens are kept in memory. Local plans and JSON backups are readable data: the app does not add encryption or password protection to them. Their protection depends on your device, browser, Google Account security, and file-sharing settings.

No method of storage or transfer is completely secure, so absolute security cannot be guaranteed. Protect access to your browser, device, and Google Account.

External services and downloaded backups

The site loads fonts from Google Fonts. Connecting Drive also loads Google's authorization library. These services receive technical information needed to handle those requests. Font requests do not include your financial plan.

Downloaded JSON backups contain readable financial information without app-provided password protection. Keep these files private. Clearing your local plan does not delete Drive backups or revoke Google access; those are separate actions described below.

Hosting logs

The hosting provider may automatically collect standard technical logs, such as IP address, browser or user agent, requested pages, timestamps, and error or status codes. These logs may be used for security, troubleshooting, and basic site operation. They do not include the financial plan stored locally in your browser.

Future analytics

The app does not currently use analytics. If privacy-respecting analytics are added later, this policy will be updated before or when that change is made.

Your choices

  • Use the app without connecting to Google Drive.
  • Use “Start over” in the Data menu to clear the current plan. To also remove saved preferences and backup metadata, clear this site's storage in your browser settings. Repeat on each browser or device where you used the app. Clearing storage can permanently remove your local plan.
  • Delete am-i-going-broke-data.json backups in Google Drive and empty Drive's trash to request permanent deletion, subject to Google's retention rules. Delete any downloaded backups separately from your device or other storage.
  • Revoke the app's Google access in your Google Account. Select this app and remove its access. Revoking access prevents further authorized access without a new grant; it does not delete existing backups or local plans.
